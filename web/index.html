Terms used on FlameLedger

Plain definitions for students and engineers new to spacecraft flammability, with the NASA source where the definition comes from a document.

Show the glossary
NASA-STD-6001 Test 1
NASA's standard upward-burning flammability test for spacecraft materials, run at the worst-case atmosphere. A sample that burns more than 15 cm fails. NTRS 20260001966, PDF p.27
ULOI (upward limiting oxygen index)
In 1g, the oxygen level at which a material passes the Test 1 burn-length criterion about half the time. NTRS 20080034883, p.2
MOC (maximum oxygen concentration)
In 1g, the highest oxygen level at which at least five samples pass the burn criterion, with at least one failing at 1% more oxygen. NTRS 20080034883, p.2
ULOI* and MOC* (low gravity)
The low-gravity versions: the lowest oxygen at which the flame survived the whole test, and the highest at which it went out. Drop-tower tests are short, so these criteria differ from 1g. NTRS 20080034883, pp.2-4 · NTRS 20130010991, p.6
Oxygen margin of safety (ΔO2)
The mean low-gravity limit minus the mean 1g limit, as defined by NASA authors. A negative value means the material burned at lower oxygen in low gravity than in the 1g test. FlameLedger shows only margins the authors published. NTRS 20080034883, p.4, Eq. 1
Blowoff limit
The oxygen level below which a flow blows the flame off the sample. The PMMA rod's 16% O2 value is a 1g blowoff limit, not a Test 1 result.
Concurrent and opposed flow
Concurrent: the air flows in the same direction the flame spreads (like upward burning on Earth). Opposed: the air flows against the spreading flame. Test-duct flows are not the same as cabin ventilation.
psia
Pounds per square inch, absolute. Earth's sea-level pressure is 14.7 psi; NASA's planned lunar habitat uses 8.2 psia, and spacesuits 4.3 psi. Challenge brief · NTRS 20260001966, PDF p.25
Microgravity, lunar and Mars gravity
Near-zero gravity in orbit (0g), the Moon's gravity (0.165 g) and Mars's gravity (0.38 g), as recorded with each ledger row.
MSG, CIR and MDCA
ISS hardware: the Microgravity Science Glovebox (MSG); the Combustion Integrated Rack (CIR), whose main feature is a 100-liter combustion chamber; and the Multi-user Droplet Combustion Apparatus (MDCA), a CIR insert for droplet experiments. NASA Glenn, Combustion Science
